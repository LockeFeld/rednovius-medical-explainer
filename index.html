Frequently Asked Questions

What is a medical explainer video?
A medical explainer video is a short animated video (typically 60–90 seconds) that distills complex clinical data, MOA information, or study results into a visual narrative designed specifically for Medical Affairs audiences — HCPs, KOLs, and payers. Unlike patient education videos, medical explainer videos maintain full scientific rigor while using animation to clarify complex mechanisms and clinical endpoints that would take pages of text to explain.
How long does production take?
5–7 business days from brief to final delivery for standard projects. Rush delivery is available for urgent congress deadlines. Full production including MLR review rounds typically completes in 8–12 weeks for complex therapeutic areas requiring multiple review cycles. We build MLR timing into the project plan from day one.
Do medical explainer videos require MLR review?
Yes — all medical explainer videos require MLR review, just like any Medical Affairs communication material. Rednovius builds MLR compliance into the process from day one. We deliver annotated scripts with full reference support and version documentation so your medical, legal, and regulatory reviewers can work efficiently without surprises or late-stage revisions.

Medical explainer videos for Medical Affairs typically range from $8,000–$18,000 depending on complexity, therapeutic area, and deliverable formats. Most clients start with a 90-second video covering one clinical study or MOA. Volume discounts apply for congress series or multi-indication portfolios. Book a demo for a specific quote tailored to your needs.
What is the difference between a medical explainer video and a visual abstract?
A visual abstract is a static infographic summary of a published study — useful for journal promotion and social media. A medical explainer video is an animated video with voiceover and motion graphics covering MOA, multiple studies, or complex clinical concepts. Videos deliver 65% better retention after 3 days vs static formats and outperform visual abstracts for congress booths, MSL meetings, and payer presentations where holding attention and communicating clinical differentiation matters most.
